THE COMPETITION RULEBOOK Match Play · Per-Hole Bets · Nassau · Presses

The committee believes every wager should be understood before the first tee shot.

01 · MATCH PLAY

Win more holes than your opponent. The lowest score on each hole wins that hole; a tie halves it. The running score is expressed as 1 UP, 2 UP, or ALL SQUARE.

The match ends when a player leads by more holes than remain. Example: 3 UP with 2 to play wins 3&2.

02 · HEAD-TO-HEAD · $ PER HOLE

Each hole is a separate wager. At $5 per hole, winning a hole earns $5, losing costs $5, and a tied hole is worth $0. The final balance is the sum of all hole results.

03 · NASSAU

Three separate matches: Front 9, Back 9, and Overall 18. A $5 Nassau typically means $5 on each match, for $15 in total initial stakes between two players.

Win the front but lose the back and overall? Your net result is minus $5.

04 · THE PRESS

A press starts an additional wager from an agreed point in the round. The original wager continues.

Example: You're 2 DOWN after five holes and press a $5 Nassau match. The new $5 match begins on hole six, while the original match keeps running.

Automatic presses, acceptance requirements, starting holes, and settlement rules vary by group. Agree on these before play.

COMMITTEE NOTE

Unless a format explicitly supports handicap adjustments, assume gross scores. House rules and money arrangements must be agreed upon before starting.

THE GAME FORMATS Bingo · Vegas · Wolf · Hammer · More

Every great game starts with everyone agreeing on the rules. The committee recommends doing this before anyone mentions money.

01 · BINGO BANGO BONGO

Three points per hole: Bingo goes to the first player on the green, Bango to the closest ball once everyone reaches the green, and Bongo to the first player to hole out. Proper playing order matters. These are separate events, not ordinary stroke-play results.

02 · VEGAS

Four players, two teams. Each team's two scores become a two-digit number, lower digit first: 4 and 5 become 45. Compare the team numbers and award the difference in points. Birdie-flip variations require a separate agreement.

03 · WOLF

Four players take turns being the Wolf. The Wolf chooses a partner for 2 versus 2 or plays alone against the other three. Partner selection timing, Lone Wolf declarations, points, and ties follow the agreed house rules.

04 · HAMMER

A team-based wagering game where a side can increase the pressure by offering a Hammer. Agree beforehand who may Hammer, when it may be used, whether it can be declined, and what happens to the stake.

05 · 6-6-6 / ROUND ROBIN

Four players rotate partners after every six holes. Each pairing plays six holes together. Agree whether each segment uses best-ball, match-play, or another scoring method and how each segment is settled.

06 · SNAKE

A putting side game. A three-putt transfers the Snake to the offending golfer. The player holding the Snake at the agreed settlement point pays the agreed amount. Multiple three-putts and tie rules should be agreed before play.

07 · SKINS

Each hole has a skin available. A sole lowest eligible score wins it. A tie for lowest normally carries the skin forward to the next hole. Decide how unclaimed skins are handled at the end of the round.

08 · STROKE PLAY

Add the player's scores across all holes. Lowest total wins. Gross uses actual strokes; net subtracts allocated handicap strokes. Ties require an agreed playoff or tie-break.

09 · STABLEFORD

A points competition based on score relative to par or net par. Standard Stableford awards zero for double bogey or worse, one for bogey, two for par, three for birdie, four for eagle, and five for albatross. Highest points wins. Modified Stableford uses different values.

OFFICIAL CLUB NOTICE

These are general explanations. Where Dagger Caddy's current scoring engine specifies particular house rules, those rules control that game. The full unified scoring system and configurable wagering rules are still in development.

TEAM GAMES & SPECIALTY FORMATS Best Ball · Scramble · Nines · Quota · More

From traditional partner competitions to questionable financial decisions, the committee has you covered.

01 · FOUR-BALL / BEST BALL

Two-player teams. Each golfer plays their own ball, and the better score from the pair counts for the team on each hole. Compare team scores in stroke play or match play. Gross and net versions differ by handicap allocation.

02 · SCRAMBLE

All teammates tee off. Choose the preferred shot, then everyone plays from that location. Repeat until the ball is holed. Record one team score per hole.

03 · SHAMBLE

All teammates tee off and select the best drive. Each golfer then plays their own ball through the remainder of the hole. Agree beforehand whether to use the best individual score or another team total.

04 · ALTERNATE SHOT

Two partners play one ball, alternating strokes until the ball is holed. Standard foursomes also alternate which partner tees off on each hole.

05 · CHAPMAN / PINEHURST

Both partners tee off, then each plays the other's ball for the second stroke. Select one ball and alternate strokes from that point until holed. Decide the order after selection correctly.

06 · NINES / 5-3-1

A three-player game. Nine points are available each hole: five to the lowest score, three to the middle, and one to the highest. Ties split the tied positions' combined points equally. Highest accumulated points wins.

07 · QUOTA / CHICAGO

Players begin with a points target determined by the group's agreed handicap method. Earn points for birdies, pars and other results. The winner is the player who beats their quota by the greatest amount.

08 · DOTS / JUNK

Award points for agreed achievements such as birdies, greenies, sandies, chip-ins and other special shots. Define each event and point value before the round. Players can earn multiple dots on the same hole.

09 · RABBIT

A hole-winning side competition. A player captures the Rabbit by winning a hole outright. A later outright winner may take it away, according to the agreed variation. Decide when the Rabbit is settled.

COMMITTEE POLICY

House rules, points, handicaps, tie-breaks and wager amounts must be agreed upon before play. These instructions do not activate unfinished scoring engines.

Bingo Bango Bongo

Three independent points on every hole. Maximum chaos, minimal mathematics.

How to play · Bingo Bango Bongo

Each hole offers three independent points:

Bingo: First player to get their ball on the green.
Bango: Closest to the hole once every ball is on the green.
Bongo: First player to hole out.

Keep proper order of play, especially for Bingo and Bongo. No points are automatically awarded for a tie; choose the applicable player or leave unawarded. It's a points game, not normal stroke-play scoring.

COMPETITION COMMITTEE · FEATURED MATCH

Vegas

Vegas · How to play

Four golfers, two fixed teams. Each team's hole score is a two-digit number built from teammates' gross strokes, lower score first (4 and 5 = 45). The lower number wins the difference in points (45 vs 56 = 11). Optional $/point turns points into money. We do not apply the optional “birdie flips the opponent's number” rule in this edition.

Wolf · How to play

Four golfers. Wolf rotates every hole (1–4 repeated). Wolf either selects one partner to play 2 vs 2 or plays Lone Wolf 1 vs 3. Score by lower best-ball gross score for each side. A team win gives each winning teammate +1 point; a losing teammate -1. Lone Wolf wins +3 and each opponent loses -1; if Lone Wolf loses, Wolf -3 and each opponent +1. Ties are zero. These are selectable house rules; confirm with your group first.

Hammer

This simplified house version records the final multiplier on each hole. If somebody hammers mid-hole, just bump the multiplier before you save.

How to play · Hammer

Two teams of two. Each hole starts at 1 unit. Any hammer during the hole increases the multiplier. At the end of the hole, record who won and what the hole was worth. Halves score zero.

COMPETITION COMMITTEE · MEMBER FAVORITE

6-6-6 / Round Robin

Better-ball teams rotate every six holes: 1–6, 7–12, then 13–18. Great for a foursome where nobody wants the same partner all day.

How to play · 6-6-6

Use four players. Teams are 1+2 vs 3+4 on holes 1–6, 1+3 vs 2+4 on holes 7–12, and 1+4 vs 2+3 on holes 13–18. Record gross strokes; the lower best-ball score wins the hole. Winning teammates receive +1 point each, losers -1. Halves are zero.
